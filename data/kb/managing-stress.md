Stress is a normal reaction to challenges, but stress that lasts a long time can affect your health. The U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) suggests managing stress a little every day, using the mix of approaches that works for you.

**Care for your mind**

- Take breaks from news and social media, since constant negative news can be upsetting.
- Make time to unwind: try deep breathing, stretching, meditation, journaling, time outdoors, or another activity you enjoy.
- Practice gratitude by writing down specific things you appreciate.
- Stay connected with people you trust and with community or faith-based groups.

**Care for your body**

- Keep a regular sleep schedule; adults should aim for at least 7 hours a night.
- Be physically active. Start small and build up toward about 2½ hours a week, for example 20–30 minutes a day.
- Eat a balanced diet with fruit, vegetables, whole grains and lean protein, and limit added sugar, salt and unhealthy fats.
- Limit alcohol or avoid it, and avoid smoking and vaping.
- Keep up with regular check-ups and screenings.

Regular physical activity is one of the most helpful things you can do for both physical and mental health.

If stress feels overwhelming or you are struggling, reach out to a health professional; in the U.S. you can also call or text 988 for free, confidential support at any time.
