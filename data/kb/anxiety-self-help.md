---
title: Self-Help for Anxiety
source: NHS Every Mind Matters — Anxiety

Feeling anxious from time to time is common. NHS Every Mind Matters suggests several self-help approaches, which tend to work best when built into a daily routine:

- **Shift your focus.** Mindfulness, meditation, breathing exercises and relaxation can ease tension by bringing your attention back to the present.
- **Try CBT-based self-help.** Techniques drawn from cognitive behavioural therapy can help you reframe worries and unhelpful thoughts and approach problems in new ways.
- **Understand your anxiety.** Keep a simple diary of what you were doing and how you felt. Over time it can reveal patterns and triggers.
- **Set aside worry time.** Pick a fixed time each day to go over your worries, so they take up less of the rest of your day.
- **Face fears gradually.** Avoiding situations, or relying on "safety" habits, can make anxiety worse. Taking small steps toward what you fear can help it feel more manageable.
- **Look at the bigger picture.** When you're stuck on details, ask what you'd say to a friend in the same situation.

If anxiety is affecting your daily life or relationships, or you're unsure whether physical symptoms such as breathlessness are due to anxiety, speak to a GP or NHS 111 (in England you can also refer yourself to NHS Talking Therapies), and get urgent help straight away if you feel unable to cope or keep yourself safe.
