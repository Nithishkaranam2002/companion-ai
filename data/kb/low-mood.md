---
title: Coping With Low Mood and Sadness
source: NHS — Low mood, sadness and depression
url: https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/feelings-symptoms-behaviours/feelings-and-symptoms/low-mood-sadness-depression/
---
Everyone feels low at times. A low mood often lifts after a few days or weeks, and small changes, such as sorting out a worry or getting more sleep, can help. It can be linked to things like work pressure, relationship or money problems, illness or bereavement, though sometimes there is no obvious cause. Talking about what is behind it can make it easier to manage.

Things the NHS suggests trying:

- Talk to someone you trust, such as a friend, family member or counsellor.
- Consider peer support, where people share experiences to help each other.
- Exercise regularly, which may lift your mood and help you sleep.
- Eat regular, healthy meals to keep your energy steadier.
- Try mindfulness, focusing on the present moment.

Things to try to avoid:

- Trying to do everything at once; set small, realistic goals instead.
- Dwelling on things you can't change; put your energy into what helps.
- Thinking you're alone; many people feel this way and support is available.
- Using alcohol, smoking, gambling or drugs to cope, as these can make you feel worse.

If your low mood has lasted two weeks or more, you're struggling to cope, or self-help isn't working, speak to a GP or a talking therapies service, and get urgent help right away if you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself.
