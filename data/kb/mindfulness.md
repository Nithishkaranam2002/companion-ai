title: Mindfulness

Mindfulness means paying attention, moment by moment, to what is happening inside and around you: your body's sensations, your surroundings, and your thoughts and feelings. It can help you notice things you usually take for granted, step back from your thoughts, and spot early signs of stress or anxiety. Thoughts can be treated as passing mental events rather than things that must control you.

Ways to be more mindful, based on NHS guidance:

- **Notice the everyday.** Pay attention to sensations while eating, walking, or touching a banister on the stairs.
- **Keep it regular.** Choose a set time each day, such as your commute or a lunchtime walk.
- **Try something new.** Sit in a different seat or go somewhere new for lunch to see things afresh.
- **Watch your thoughts.** The aim is not to push them away but to let them pass. If your mind is very busy, gentle yoga or walking may help.
- **Name thoughts and feelings.** Silently labelling them, such as "this is worry", can build awareness.
- **Come back to the present** whenever you notice you've drifted into the past or future.

More formal practice includes sitting quietly and focusing on your breath, sounds, or body, as well as yoga and tai chi. Mindfulness helps many people, but not everyone, and some people find it makes them feel worse.

If mindfulness isn't helping or difficult feelings are affecting your daily life, consider speaking to a GP or another health professional.
