Getting enough water every day matters for health. According to the CDC, not drinking enough can lead to dehydration, which can contribute to unclear thinking, mood changes, overheating, constipation and kidney stones. Water also helps regulate body temperature, cushions joints, protects sensitive tissues, and helps the body get rid of waste. You need more water in hot weather, when you're more active, or when you're unwell with a fever, vomiting or diarrhea.

How much you need varies by age, sex, activity level and other factors. Most of it comes from drinks, but water-rich foods like many fruits and vegetables count too.

Tips for drinking more water:

- Carry a reusable water bottle.
- Freeze some water in freezer-safe bottles for cold water through the day.
- Choose water instead of sugary drinks, and ask for water when eating out.
- Serve water with meals.
- Add a wedge of lemon or lime for flavor.

Healthier choices include plain water, sparkling water, unsweetened coffee and tea, and milk or unsweetened fortified alternatives. Sugary drinks like regular soda, sweetened coffees and teas, fruit drinks, and sports and energy drinks add calories with little nutrition, so it's best to limit them. If you drink alcohol, do so in moderation.

If you often feel very thirsty, dizzy, or unwell despite drinking enough, check in with a health care provider.
