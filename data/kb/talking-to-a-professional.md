title: Talking With a Health Care Provider About Your Mental Health
source: NIMH — Tips for Talking With a Health Care Provider About Your Mental Health

It can feel hard to bring up your mental health, but the U.S. National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH) offers tips to make the conversation easier:

- **Start with your primary care provider.** If you're not sure where to begin, your regular doctor, nurse practitioner or physician assistant can help and can refer you to a mental health professional. Don't wait for them to ask — raise it yourself.
- **Prepare ahead.** Appointments are often short. Write down your questions and concerns beforehand, and if you can, find out about any mental health history in your family.
- **Bring someone along if it helps.** A trusted friend or relative can offer support, take notes and help you remember what was said.
- **Be honest and specific.** Conversations with your provider are private. Describe what you've been experiencing, when it started, how often it happens and how much it affects you, and mention any big stresses or life changes.
- **Ask questions.** Ask for more information about anything you don't understand. If a suggested approach doesn't feel right, say so and ask about other options; you can also seek a second opinion.

No single approach works for everyone, so it can take time, and sometimes more than one provider, to find what fits you.

If you have been struggling for two weeks or more, or your feelings are getting in the way of daily life, it is a good time to make that appointment.
