---
title: Mental Health at Work
source: WHO — Mental health at work
url: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/mental-health-at-work
---
The World Health Organization notes that decent work can support good mental health by providing income, a sense of purpose and achievement, social connection and daily structure. Poor working conditions, on the other hand, can put mental health at risk.

Common workplace risks WHO highlights include heavy workloads or a fast pace, long or inflexible hours, little control over your work, unclear roles, job insecurity, poor support from colleagues or managers, conflicting home and work demands, and bullying, harassment or discrimination.

What can help, according to WHO:

- **Organisations** can reduce risks at their source, for example through flexible working arrangements and clear ways of dealing with harassment, and can involve workers in decisions that affect them.
- **Managers** benefit from training to recognise and respond to colleagues in distress, using open communication and active listening.
- **Workers** can build mental health awareness and stress-management skills, and leisure-time physical activity can help too.
- **Reasonable adjustments** can support people with mental health conditions, such as flexible hours, extra time for tasks, adjusted duties to reduce stress, time off for appointments, or regular supportive check-ins with a supervisor.
- **Return-to-work support**, like a phased return, can help after a mental health-related absence.

If work stress is affecting your health or daily life, consider talking to your manager, an occupational health service, or a health professional.
