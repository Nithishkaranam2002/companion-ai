title: Alcohol, Mood and Cutting Down

Alcohol can affect how you feel. The NHS notes that heavy drinking is strongly linked with depression, hangovers can leave you feeling anxious and low, and if you're already feeling anxious or sad, drinking can make it worse. Alcohol may help some people fall asleep, but it disrupts sleep and can stop you getting deep sleep. Cutting down can help improve your mood and leave you feeling more rested.

Practical tips from the NHS for drinking less:

- **Make a plan.** Decide on a limit before you start drinking.
- **Set a budget.** Only take a fixed amount of money to spend on drinks.
- **Let people know.** Tell friends and family you're cutting down so they can support you.
- **Take it a day at a time.** Cut back a little each day and count every day you stick to your goal as a success.
- **Choose smaller or weaker drinks**, such as a bottle instead of a pint or a small glass of wine, or a lower-strength option.
- **Stay hydrated.** Eat and have a glass of water before drinking, and alternate alcoholic drinks with soft drinks.
- **Take a break.** Have several drink-free days each week.

If you're finding it hard to cut down, feel you need to drink, or drinking is affecting your mood or life, talk to a GP or an alcohol support service — and don't stop suddenly if you drink heavily every day without getting medical advice first.
